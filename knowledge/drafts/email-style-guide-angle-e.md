Here's what they all have in common:

Intro.

Three bullet points.

Tidy conclusion.

Transitions so smooth they feel like they were written by a committee of robots who've never been to a party — the kind of party where someone brings store-bought hummus and calls it "catering."

Technically fine. Spiritually dead.

Like oatmeal. Nutritious. Forgettable. You eat it because it's there, not because you're excited about it.

The info might even be good. Useful, even. Nobody cares. Because nothing in there is *fun* to read.

No rhythm. No sass. No sticky line that rattles around your skull on the drive home.

No weird specific detail — like the guy eating a tuna sandwich during a cold call, or spilling coffee on your 2007 MacBook in pizza-print pajamas.

No joke that lands. Even a tiny one. (The bar is on the floor. And they're still tripping.)

No open loop that makes your brain go *wait, what happened next?*

Just… intro. Three points. Conclusion. Swipe left on life.

And here's the part that should annoy you:

That's not a *you* problem.

That's what happens when everyone uses AI the same way. Same skeleton. Same structure. Same polite little transitions between paragraphs — like everyone's writing from the same sad template in the sky.

Reddit's calling it out. People say AI writing is "super formulaic — intro, three main points, conclusion, every single time."

Sound familiar? Yeah. Because you just read five of them. And your readers are doing the same thing to *your* emails.

Let that sink in.

---

So what's missing?

Not better information. You've got information coming out of your ears. You're drowning in value. Gasping for entertainment.

What's missing is what I call **secondary rewards**.

The sassy stuff. The sticky stuff. The stuff that makes someone keep reading even after they already got the point:

- **Rhythm and sound play** — sassy sales scripts that sell without sounding slimy. Busy bees buzzing about business. (Cheesy on purpose. Still works.)
- **Weird comparisons** — selling without a plan is like baking a cake without a recipe. Messy. Disappointing. Probably smoke alarm involved.
- **Hyper-specific details** — not "I had a bad sales call." I cold-called 37 people and got rejected by everyone except the guy eating a tuna sandwich.
- **Open loops** — I'll tell you the weirdest trick I learned… but first, a quick story. Your brain hates unfinished business. Use that.
- **Humor that disarms** — I'd rather wrestle a raccoon for the last slice of pizza than read another three-point AI email. (Hyperbolic? Sure. Did you keep reading? You're welcome.)

Information informs.

Secondary rewards persuade.

Nobody ever bought because your tutorial was thorough. They kept reading because it was *fun*. Or at least not boring. There's a difference. A big one.

Boring is the only unforgivable sin in copy. And right now, most AI emails are committing it in broad daylight.
